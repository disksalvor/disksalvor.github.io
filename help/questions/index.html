Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No trial, no file or size limits, no paid edition, no account. It's open source under the MIT licence.

Where is the source code?

It will be on GitHub at github.com/disksalvor after the current round of testing on real failing drives, under the MIT licence: free to use, read, change and share.

Can it make my drive worse?

DiskSalvor never writes to the drive it rescues. Reading a failing drive does wear it, which is why DiskSalvor reads as little as possible: weak areas last, nothing twice, rests when the drive struggles, and a stop when it's getting worse.

Will it repair my drive?

No, and on purpose: with failing hardware, the files come first. Once they're safe, the report says what to do with the drive (replace it, or test it before trusting it again).

Why does Windows ask for permission?

Windows only lets programs read a whole drive with an administrator's OK. DiskSalvor asks only when you pick a drive, and explains why first. Windows' prompt names Python, the language DiskSalvor is written in. Before that, the first time you open it, Windows may say it doesn't recognise the publisher, because DiskSalvor isn't signed yet: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Mac or Linux?

Windows only for now. Other platforms are on the roadmap, and help is welcome.

When can I download it?

After the current round of testing on real failing drives. Follow the project on GitHub, or write to us, to hear when it's out.

Anything else: hello@disksalvor.com. For a drive that's failing now, see what to do first.
